# CFO note — October 23, 2026

The company is profitable and cash-positive at the October 23 defense cutoff. Company revenue is $41,000, gross profit is $16,400, net income is $10,300, and ending cash is $120,300. All balance-sheet, cash-flow, consolidation, and ledger-completeness controls pass.

The October 16 update raised company revenue from $37,000 to $41,000. The main drivers were Utica's newly supported $5,000 credit sale and the $1,000 reduction in Scranton's credit sale after the controller correction. Gross profit increased by $1,600 and net income increased by $600. Ending cash increased by $4,600 as additional receipts were recognized.

Ending cash decreased by $500: Scranton purchased $3,000 of installed office equipment while Utica collected $2,500 from a customer. These later items explain why profit, cash, and receivables do not all move together.

Utica has the highest revenue and net income, but it also has the largest ending receivables balance at $15,500. Management should review collections and credit follow-up; the available evidence does not include receivables aging or payment terms beyond the individual source records. Nashua has the highest operating-expense ratio, driven by its documented occupancy and delivery costs. Because every branch has a 40.0% gross margin, the meaningful operating differences are sales volume and operating expenses rather than gross margin.

The reporting package keeps corrections, duplicate evidence, late confirmations, proposals, and prospective orders visible in the exception register. No unapproved equipment proposal or prospective customer order has been recorded as a transaction. Management should use the exception register and branch input-quality actions to direct refresher training on source-document matching, duplicate detection, and approval before posting.
